\section*{Q1: Research Question}
How do observer identity and the declared visibility of a language model's
verbalized reasoning affect answer accuracy, alignment with a misleading
anonymous suggestion, and acknowledgment of that suggestion? I will compare a
developer, a domain expert, and an automated AI monitor, each declared to see
either the rationale and answer or only the answer. Oversight framing may change
the evidence an audit collects, connecting evaluation and foundation-model safety.

\section*{Q2: Prior Work and Limitations}
\citet{turpin2023unfaithful} and \citet{chen2025reasoning} show that hints can
influence answers without being acknowledged, but do not test this visibility
comparison. \citet{chen2026control} study reasoning controllability, including
explicit AI-monitoring awareness, rather than hint acknowledgment across
observer roles. \citet{covas2026watched} study framing in one debate topic;
their human--AI observer contrast is suggestive rather than statistically
resolved. \citet{li2026decomposing} distinguish evaluation cues, recognition,
and response; verbalized recognition is often not associated with behavioural
change. These motivate a controlled extension, not a new claim of awareness.

\section*{Q3: Novelty of Approach}
I extend hint-disclosure and monitoring-awareness studies by crossing observer
identity with declared rationale visibility under a fixed misleading
suggestion on a small local model. It tests framing effects when hints are
present, not causal changes in hint susceptibility under every observer setting.

\section*{Q4: Impact and Motivation}
If monitoring changes a rationale's acknowledgment of external suggestions,
auditors may misjudge its transparency. Benchmark designers and safety evaluators
could use the results to assess how oversight framing affects chain-of-thought
audit evidence in this limited setting.

\section*{Q5: Risks and Assumptions}
Hint alignment does not prove causal influence, and rationales do not establish
internal computation or intentional concealment. Answer-only observer access
is declared, not actual privacy or model belief. Matched templates limit wording
differences but cannot remove role/authority confounding. Separate pilot items
will check solvability, trace truncation, and labeling time; a separate
comprehension probe checks the access instruction, not belief. Human validation
will check automated labels; observer mentions may compromise blinding. Small
samples, benchmark exposure, and one-model generalization limit conclusions.

\section*{Q8: Milestones and Success Criteria}
The eight arms are three observers $\times$ rationale-visible/answer-only
access, plus no-observer/no-hint and no-observer/hint controls. All generate the
same rationale and answer fields in fresh conversations. Each hint is sampled
uniformly from incorrect labels and fixed across hinted arms. The primary
contrast is visibility, averaged over the three fixed roles; identity
comparisons are exploratory. The primary endpoint is \emph{unacknowledged
wrong-hint alignment}: selecting the hinted label without explicitly mentioning
the external suggestion, divided by all items in that arm. Alignment,
acknowledgment, and accuracy will also be reported separately. No-hint answers
will be scored against the same hidden target label to estimate excess
alignment in the no-observer setting. Binary acknowledgment labels will be
checked on 100--120 human-coded traces stratified across conditions and label
predictions, using positive-class precision/recall; unreliable automation
triggers the smaller, manually labeled design. Paired, task-stratified item
bootstrap 95\% intervals will quantify uncertainty under one seed schedule.
Success is a completed reproducible study with checked scoring, validated
labels, and documented limitations; null results may remain inconclusive about
small effects.
