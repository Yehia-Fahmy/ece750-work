{\Large\textbf{ECE750T: Research Project Proposal}}\\[5pt]
{\large\textbf{Reasoning Under Observation: Observer Effects on LLM Rationales}}\\[4pt]
Yehia Fahmy \quad | \quad 20786266\\
University of Waterloo \quad | \quad Fall 2026
\vspace{0.5em}\hrule\vspace{0.5em}

\section*{Q1: Research Question}
How do observer identity and the declared visibility of a language model's
verbalized reasoning affect its accuracy, susceptibility to an anonymous
suggested answer, and disclosure of that influence? I will compare a developer,
a domain expert, and an automated AI monitor when each is said to see either the
model's rationale and answer or only its answer. This connects evaluation,
auditing, and foundation-model safety: if announced oversight changes behaviour,
an audit may not predict deployment.

\section*{Q2: Prior Work and Limitations}
\citet{covas2026watched} report that human and AI monitoring prompts change
style, but study one debate topic rather than correctness or disclosure.
\citet{feng2026peoplepleasers} find that reasoning can reduce sycophancy while
masking it through post-hoc rationalization, but do not vary who observes it.
\citet{li2026decomposing} separate evaluation cues, recognition, and behavioural
response; recognition often causes no response. \citet{chen2026control} find
that models control outputs more readily than reasoning traces.
\citet{turpin2023unfaithful} show that suggested answers can influence
predictions without acknowledgment. I combine these concerns in one audit.

\section*{Q3: Novelty of Approach}
Prior work separately studies observer identity, evaluation awareness,
sycophancy, and reasoning controllability. I will cross observer identity with
declared rationale visibility while holding an anonymous suggestion fixed.
Unlike style-based observer studies, I measure correctness and disclosure;
unlike model-organism studies, I require no training or deceptive objective.

\section*{Q4: Impact and Motivation}
Chain-of-thought is proposed as audit evidence. If monitoring changes a
rationale without comparably changing the answer---or encourages silent
conformity---auditors may overestimate transparency. The results could guide
benchmark designers deciding when verbalized reasoning is useful evidence.

\section*{Q5: Risks and Assumptions}
Generated rationales are not guaranteed to reveal internal computation, so I
will not claim intentional concealment. ``Declared-private'' describes the
prompt, not actual privacy or belief. Observer wording may confound identity
with authority; matched templates and a second paraphrase will test this. A
pilot will avoid floor/ceiling tasks; blinded human coding will check automated
labels. Benchmark exposure and one-model generalization remain limitations.

\section*{Q6: Resources}
I will use 100--150 public BIG-Bench Hard multiple-choice items
\citep{suzgun2023challenging} from three tasks selected by a pilot, plus a
random anonymous answer suggestion per item. The
primary model will be Qwen3-4B in Q4\_K\_M quantization, run through
\texttt{llama.cpp} at 4K--8K context and batch size one. Its 2.5\,GB weights and
approximately 4\,GB runtime footprint fit my 8\,GB RTX 3070 Ti. Python will
generate prompts, parse answers, and score results; no training is required.

\section*{Q8: Milestones and Success Criteria}
The eight conditions are three observers $\times$ visible/declared-private
rationale, a no observer/no suggestion baseline, and an anonymous hint-only
control. Suggested labels are random; analyses of influence focus on items where
the suggestion is wrong. Primary outcomes are accuracy, following the wrong
suggestion, and ``silent conformity'' (following it without acknowledgment).
Secondary outcomes include answer flips, uncertainty language, observer
mentions, and rationale--answer consistency. Answers will be scored
deterministically. Rationale categories will be assigned automatically or by an
LLM judge, then checked against blinded human labels on a stratified subset.
I will report paired bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals and McNemar tests for
binary answer changes. Success means a reproducible pipeline, a documented
manipulation check, validated rationale coding, and bounded effect estimates;
a precise null result is informative because it limits observer effects in this
setting.
